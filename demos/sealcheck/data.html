Reference - what a counter check can actually see

Six things you can read
without a laboratory.

A number, a date, a glue seam, a foil, a dial and a receipt. That is the whole instrument panel available to a buyer in a chemist or an open market, and every table on this page exists to make one of those six readable in seconds.

    01 · What a counter check proves

    Six hard numbers, and the honest limit of each

    02 · Registration numbers

    The documented shapes, what each one is supposed to be printed on, and the trap in it

    A registration number is text, and text copies. What still fails is the shape: the letter, the digit count, the dash and the suffix. Learn these five rules and most lazy fakes fall out before you ask a single question.

    As printed on the pack Body digits What it should be on What the checker returns The trap in it
    A-123455Imported finished product registered through a Nigerian agentRecognised shapeCopies as easily as any other text; verify the entry against this exact product, strength and pack
    F-123455Finished product manufactured in NigeriaRecognised shapeAn F- number on a pack whose text says imported means one of the two is wrong - ask which factory
    A-1234566The older documented length for an imported productRecognised, flaggedSix-digit bodies are more often legacy or dormant listings than current ones
    F-1234566The older documented length for a local productRecognised, flaggedSame caveat: the shape passing tells you less here, not more
    A-12345CR5Contract or repack-related registration, seen against bulk handlingValid shape, wrong contextA CR suffix never belongs on a sealed retail carton; ask for the finished-product number
    A-12345X5Bulk material or active pharmaceutical ingredient registrationValid shape, wrong contextX appears on drums and material records, not on patient packs
    12/45674 + 4Older 5-6 digit numeric form, often written nn/nnnnRecognised as legacyNo letter prefix at all, so ask which agent imported it and whether the listing is still live
    456785Plain legacy number, no prefixRecognised as legacyAlso exactly what a copied label looks like when the copier dropped the letter
    4567896Plain legacy number, long formRecognised as legacyFrequently a batch code being read as a registration number; check which field you are actually looking at
    A-12344Nothing documentedNot a valid shapeDocumented bodies are 5-6 digits, so a 4-digit body is rejected on the shape alone
    A-12345677Nothing documentedNot a valid shapeLonger than any documented form - usually a barcode or a shelf code being misread
    A123455Nothing documentedNot a valid shapeGenuine packs print the dash; a missing dash is a bad transcription or a bad copy
    B-123455No letter prefix this tool knowsNot a valid shapeDocumented retail prefixes are A (imported) and F (locally manufactured), nothing else
    no number, or illegible0Nothing legal at retailStopThe registration check is fatal in this tool: a missing or unreadable number is a stop, not a question

    Shapes are the ones commonly documented and reported on packs. Regulators add and retire formats, and the official register is the only authority - this page queries nothing and never returns a lookup result.

    03 · Seals and factory finishes

    Which pack finishes copy well, and which ones a counterfeiter cannot carry out of the shop

    Copyable means the finish can be bought or reproduced and should never carry weight on its own. Nearly impossible means the factory equipment is the barrier, which is why the physical checks are weighted heavier than the printed ones.

    04 · Storage bands and heat damage

    What heat does to each dosage form, and how fast it does it

    Heat damage is invisible in a liquid and permanent in an antigen. The degree-hour figures below are this tool's own screening guides: deviation from the band, multiplied by hours, summed across every event the pack has been through since it was made.

    Dosage form Instruction printed on the pack Band to hold Freeze damage What heat does, invisibly Screening guide, degree-hours What to do at the counter

    Insulin glargine or soluble, unopened2-8 C, protect from light2-8A frozen vial is discarded, not shaken and reusedLoss of potency appears as unexplained high blood sugar, never in the vialwarn 240, discard 480Only glargine and soluble are clear and colourless; cloudiness in either is a defect, and NPH is the exception
    Insulin in use, at room temperatureIn use below about 30 C for a limited number of days2-30Not applicable once in useThe in-use clock runs while you argue about the printed date480 discard bandWrite the date of first use on the pen; 28 days governs, some presentations 42
    Antibiotic syrup requiring refrigeration2-8 C after reconstitution2-8Uncommon, but separation after a cold spell is a rejectThe clock after reconstitution is 7-14 days, not the printed expirywarn 72, discard 144Ask the day the water was added; at 14 days the carton date no longer applies
    Oxytetracycline 20% injection, veterinary15-25 C, shake before use15-25Grey precipitate forms after freezing and never redissolvesParticulates grow, and the vial neck cracks on the waywarn 60, discard 150Hold it to the light; grey, gritty or cracked is a discard whatever the paperwork says
    ORS plus zinc kitBelow 30 C, keep dry2-30Not applicableMoisture ingress: caking, salt crust, sachets that no longer stand firmwarn 200, discard 500An in-date pack with a caked sachet is still a reject, and expired ORS is a hard stop

    Degree-hour guides are the tool's own illustrative screening budgets, drawn from the shape of WHO excursion guidance for these classes and editable in the inspector. They are not product-specific stability data and they never overrule the manufacturer's insert or your state EPI officer.

    05 · Which clock governs

    Shelf life is not the same as an in-use life

    For several classes the printed expiry stops governing the moment the pack is opened or reconstituted. Rows marked hard stop are the classes this tool refuses on the date rather than negotiating about it.

    Dry syrup, unreconstituted24 months7-14 days refrigerated once water is addedConversation until you add waterThe moment it is reconstituted it becomes the row below, not this one
    Reconstituted antibiotic syrup7-14 days refrigerated from the day of reconstitutionSame clock, and it is the only one that mattersHard stop at the 14th dayPast 14 days the printed expiry no longer applies: discard, even if the carton says 2027

    ORS plus zinc24 months sealed and dryOne sachet, one dissolution; the rest is discardedHard stopExpired ORS is a hard stop, not a soft one: a rehydration failure in a child is not a rounding error
    InsulinTo the printed date at 2-8 C28 days from first use, some presentations 42Hard stop on the in-use clockIn use, the 28-day clock governs and the printed date stops governing

    Ranges are what a genuine pack of that class is expected to show, used as a sanity check on the date in front of you. The insert inside the carton is the authority, and where the two disagree follow the insert.

    08 · Three places a passing pack is still dangerous

    The score is not the verdict you want

    Under-dosing is invisible

    A wrong API, an under-dose, heavy metals and microbial contamination pass every visual check on this page. That is why a report with a batch number is worth more than a score.

    A valid number is copyable text

    Every format rule here is public, which means it is printable. The shape decides whether you ask; the register decides whether it is true, and a pharmacist has to be the one to open it.

    Unsure is not safe

    An unsure answer carries 0.4 credit instead of letting you off, and coverage below 100 percent marks the score provisional. Six unsure answers is a worse pack than four No answers, not a better one.

    09 · Where to verify what is on this page

    Open these before you act on a number

    • The official NAFDAC register and its published alerts, recall notices and complaint route: the register is the only authority on a registration number, and this tool never queries it.
    • The manufacturer's insert inside the carton: it overrules every storage band, degree-hour guide and in-use clock on this page.
    • The WHO guidance behind vaccine vial monitors and cold-chain excursion management, for the stage readings and the reporting duty.
    • The GS1 prefix list, for any barcode prefix that matters; the ranges here are the ones the inspector carries, with confidence marked.
    • Your state drug officer or state pharmacist, who can inspect the outlet and lift the whole batch rather than your single pack.
    • The licensed pharmacist or superintendent at the point of sale, who carries the duty to quarantine stock and can test the number from the shop.
