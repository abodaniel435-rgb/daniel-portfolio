    03 · Battery chemistry

    The decision that sets your real cost per year

    Capacity is not energy. A tubular battery rated 10 kWh will give you about 5 kWh, cycle for two to three years, and need topping up with distilled water. Lithium gives you 90% of the rating and ten times the cycles, for roughly three times the price.

    05 · Reading a quote like an installer

    Three lines that decide whether you were sold a system or a story

    The battery is quoted in Ah, not kWh

    100 Ah at 24 V is 2.4 kWh, and half of it is usable. Ask for the usable number and the chemistry, in writing.

    The array is sized for the day, not the night

    If the panels are chosen to cover daytime watts and the battery only covers 'backup', the system will hand you the generator at 9pm.

    The five-year fuel line is missing

    The honest comparison is system price plus five years of servicing against five years of petrol and generator maintenance. SunCost puts both columns on one screen.

    06 · Check these against

    Where to verify before you pay

    • Your own meter readings for two weeks — the only load profile that describes your house.
    • Nigerian solar-resource maps and the Global Solar Atlas for peak sun hours at your coordinates.
    • The panel and battery datasheets for the exact models in your quote: Voc, Isc, DoD, cycle count.
    • Your NEPA/DisCo tariff band and the last three bills, if grid charging is part of the plan.
    • Two more quotes for the same specification — the spread tells you more than any single number.
