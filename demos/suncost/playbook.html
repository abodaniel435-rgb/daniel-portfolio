Playbook · before you pay a deposit

The system you buy is
the specification you wrote.

Installers sell what they stock. If you arrive with a load list, a battery requirement and a commissioning test, you buy what you need instead. This is the sequence that gets you there.

On this page

    01 · The buying sequence

    Nine steps, in this order

    Skip step one and every later step becomes a negotiation. Do step one properly and the quotes compare themselves.

    1. Write the load list

      Every appliance, its watts, its hours, whether it runs at night. Two weeks of meter readings beats any guess.

      This is the only step nobody can do for you.

    2. Split day load from night load

      Panels pay for daylight. Only the battery pays for 9pm. The two numbers size two different products.

    3. Set the autonomy you are buying

      Choose it in hours, not adjectives: 'fridge, fans, lights and router until 7am' is a requirement. 'Full backup' is a marketing term.

    4. Compute the array, inverter and bank yourself

      Run SunCost. You now know the kWh, the peak watts and the usable battery you need — before anybody quotes you.

    5. Ask for the specification, not a price

      Send three installers the same written spec: array kWp, panel model, inverter kVA, battery kWh usable, chemistry, mounting, cabling, labour.

    6. Compare the quotes line by line

      Same spec, different price, is a margin conversation. Different spec, same price, is a warning.

    7. Agree the commissioning test before the deposit

      Written: a night with no grid and no generator, the agreed load connected, the system carrying it to the agreed hour.

    8. Supervise the install

      Panel angle and orientation, string sizing, breaker and surge protection, battery room ventilation, torque on the terminals, cable sizing.

    9. Do the test, then pay the balance

      The balance is your only leverage. Pay it after the test passes, with the datasheets and warranty cards in hand.

    02 · Where the money leaks

    Seven things that go wrong, in the order they happen

    • Sizing by 'how many rooms'. Rooms do not draw watts. A two-bedroom flat with an AC and a freezer needs a bigger system than a four-bedroom with fans.
    • Quoting battery Ah without volts. 200 Ah at 12 V is 2.4 kWh. At 48 V it is 9.6 kWh. The Ah number alone tells you nothing.
    • Usable capacity hidden. Lead-acid at 50% depth of discharge means half the rating is decoration.
    • No surge headroom for pumps and freezers. The system runs fine until the borehole pump starts, then it trips every morning.
    • Panels facing the wrong way or flat. A 15-degree error in orientation is a permanent 10-20% cut in output, paid for by you every day of the system's life.
    • Undersized cabling and no surge protection. Cheap to fix at install, expensive as a fire, and the lightning claim is declined when the protection was never fitted.
    • Warranty on the panel, none on the workmanship. Ask who fixes a leaking roof penetration in the rainy season, and get it in writing.

    03 · Before the deposit

    The pre-payment checklist

    • The spec is written by you. Array kWp, inverter kVA, usable battery kWh, chemistry.
    • Three quotes, same specification. And the spread between them is under 25%.
    • Panel and battery model numbers are on the quote. Not '100Ah tubular' — the make, the model, the datasheet.
    • The commissioning test is in the agreement. With the load, the hour and the pass condition written down.
    • Mounting, cabling, breakers and surge protection are line items. Anything not itemised is where the value engineering happens.
    • The balance is payable after the test. Not after installation, not after 'satisfaction'.

    04 · Owning it

    The maintenance year

    A solar system is the lowest-maintenance power source you can buy in Nigeria — and it is not zero. This is the calendar that keeps the output where the datasheet says it should be.

    Monthly

    Wash the panels, look at the roof

    Dust and bird droppings cost more output than a faulty inverter. Check for new penetrations and cable chafe.

    Monthly (tubular)

    Check the water level

    Distilled water only, in the afternoon, never overfill. Low plates kill a tubular battery in one hot week.

    Quarterly

    Read the inverter log

    Fault codes, low-voltage cutouts, overload trips. Three months of logs tells you whether the system was sized correctly.

    Quarterly

    Torque check and thermal scan

    Loose terminals are the most common cause of a burnt combiner box. Feel the inverters and breakers after a hot afternoon.

    Yearly

    Capacity test the bank

    Full charge, then run the agreed load to cutoff and time it. Compare with the commissioning figure — that difference is your degradation.

    Before harmattan

    Clean and inspect

    Dust season is coming. Sealed enclosures, gaskets and the panel angle matter most in December and January.

    05 · Questions buyers ask

    Straight answers

    How many panels do I need to run a fridge and three fans?

    Start with the watt-hours, not the appliances. A fridge and three fans is roughly 4,500-5,400 Wh a day, which at 5 peak sun hours with 15% losses needs about 1.1 kW of panels and — if you want it at night — 3-4 kWh of usable battery. Put your own hours into SunCost before you accept a quote.

    Is lithium worth three times the price?

    Usually yes, and the arithmetic is simple: lithium gives you 90% of the rated capacity for eight to ten years, tubular gives you 50% for two to three. Per usable kWh per year, lithium is often cheaper. It also survives being left at 40% for a week.

    Can I add panels later?

    Yes if the inverter has the input headroom and the battery is sized for the extra charge current. Say so at purchase, because a 3.5 kVA hybrid inverter bought 'to save money' is the reason you cannot expand later.

    Why does my system die at 9pm when the battery says 60%?

    Because the usable capacity is the middle of the curve, the inverter cutoff is set too high, or the bank is older than the label claims. Do the capacity test in the playbook and you will know which.

    Should I keep the generator?

    Keep it for the week that never happens — the funeral, the rainy week, the fault. Size the solar for normal life, not for the worst day of the year, and the payback stays honest.

    Does solar work in Port Harcourt?

    At 4.2 peak sun hours you need about 25% more array than the same house in Ibadan, and more battery because the rainy weeks are real. SunCost has the city in its list — use it instead of an Ibadan quote.

    What is a fair price per kWh installed?

    In 2026, a delivered-and-installed lithium system lands around ₦360,000-₦450,000 per usable kWh including panels and labour, and a tubular system around ₦150,000-₦200,000 per rated kWh. Anything far below is being paid for somewhere you cannot see.

    Can I connect to the grid and sell my power?

    Not for a household system today. Treat the grid as a free charging source when it appears, and check whether your DisCo's meter allows export before anybody tells you it does.

    06 · Words on the quote

    Glossary

    Peak sun hour (PSH)
    One hour at 1,000 W/m2. An annual average used to turn irradiation into expected energy.
    kWp
    Kilowatt-peak — the array's rated output in laboratory conditions, about 75-85% of what you get in a Nigerian afternoon.
    Depth of discharge (DoD)
    The share of a battery's rated capacity you may use. 50% for lead-acid, 80-90% for lithium.
    C-rate
    Charge or discharge speed relative to capacity. Lithium tolerates higher C-rates; lead-acid heats and loses capacity.
    Hybrid inverter
    One box that manages panels, battery and grid or generator input, and switches between them.
    MPPT
    Maximum power point tracking — the charger that squeezes the most watts out of the array as the sun moves.
    kVA vs kW
    kVA is apparent power. An inverter rated 3.5 kVA at 0.8 power factor carries 2.8 kW of real load.
    String
    Panels wired in series to raise voltage. Too few and the MPPT cannot work; too many and the inverter cuts out at midday heat.
    Autonomy
    How long the battery alone carries the load. Say it in hours, with the load named.
    Soiling
    Dust, pollen and droppings on the glass. The cheapest energy loss to fix and the most commonly ignored.
    BMS
    Battery management system — the board that balances cells and cuts the circuit before damage. Without it, lithium is a fire risk.
    Crossover month
    The month when cumulative solar cost falls below cumulative generator cost. SunCost computes it for your own numbers.
