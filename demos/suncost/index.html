01 · The money you already spend

Nobody budgets for fuel. That is the problem.

A generator has no invoice you can plan against — it costs ₦4,000 here, ₦6,500 there, and by December it has eaten the price of a good solar system. Solar quotes go the other way: one big number, no explanation, and a battery bank that dies at 11pm because the installer never asked what you run at night.

A fridge that cannot stop. One 6-hour outage spoils insulin and vaccines; the loss is not the fuel bill, it is the stock — so the backup-critical load is the whole design brief.

Financing: PAYG and instalments

SunKing-style pay-as-you-go is not cheaper — it is cheaper per week than petrol while costing more in total. The table shows both truths at once.

Annuity maths: payment = balance × rate per period ÷ (1 − (1 + rate)−periods). Asset ownership at the end of the term is assumed — check the exit clause in any real PAYG contract.

Financing & replacement assumptions
What this tool does not do
  • No shading, soiling or orientation model — a west-facing roof with a mango tree over it will not give you the annual average.
  • No load diversity. Peak load is every appliance on at once, which oversizes the inverter; a real installer applies a diversity factor you cannot audit.
  • No grid feed-in or net metering income. Most DISCOs will not buy your surplus; if yours will, treat it as a bonus, not a plan.
  • No guarantee on sun hours, no weather risk, no battery degradation curve beyond the replacement interval.
  • No inverter harmonics, earthing design, changeover isolation or roof-load certification — all of which are physical safety matters, not arithmetic.
  • No SunKing or Lumar price list. The PAYG rate is your input, not their quotation.
Why my inverter looks too small for my panels

Because the two are sized by different things. The inverter rating comes from your simultaneous peak load; the array comes from your daily energy. A house that uses 9 kWh a day but never draws more than 800 W needs a small inverter and a big array — which is exactly why cheap "5 kVA for everything" quotes feel oversized and yet still die at night. SunCost prices the smaller of the two requirements but warns you when the array exceeds what that inverter can accept.
