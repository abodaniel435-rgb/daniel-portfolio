How the bands work

A reference range, not a single price.

Every area and property type has a low, a median and a high — because two flats on the same street rarely go for the same money.

Example · 2 Bedroom Flat, Yaba median —
Below the band · good deal Within 8% · fair 8–25% over · watch 25%+ over · overpriced

Bands come from the RentJury reference dataset for each area and property type. They describe the rental market, not a valuation of the specific flat — condition, security and servicing still move a price.

  1. 01

    We place your quote on the band

    Your annual rent is measured against the median for that exact area and property type. A 2-bedroom in Yaba runs ₦1.12m – ₦1.96m, with a ₦1.54m median.

  2. 02

    We grade the gap

    Within 8% of the median is fair. Between 8% and 25% above is watch it. More than 25% above is overpriced. Below the range? A good deal — but inspect carefully, because cheap usually hides a reason.

  3. 03

    We check the fee separately

    Agency and legal fees are capped at 10% of annual rent in the widely accepted standard. Caution deposits above 50% get flagged. A fair rent with a stacked fee is still a bad deal.

  4. 04

    You get a next step, not just a colour

    Every verdict carries guidance: what to counter with, what to ask in writing, and when to walk. A number is only useful if you know what to do with it.

How the bands are built

Each of the 48 covered areas has a multiplier against a baseline rent for every property type. Lagos is covered at neighbourhood level — from Egbeda at the baseline through to Ikoyi at roughly four times it. Every other state is covered at its capital or main commercial hub.

Those multipliers produce a low, median and high annual rent for each area and property type. They are reference bands, built to describe a market, not a formal valuation of a specific flat.

Grading is deliberately simple:

  • Fair — within 8% of the area median.
  • Watch it — 8% to 25% above the median.
  • Overpriced — more than 25% above the median.
  • Below the band — a good deal, but inspect for the reason.

Fees are checked separately: agency and legal fees against a 10% ceiling each, caution deposits against a 50% watch line. None of this replaces reading a tenancy agreement before you sign.
