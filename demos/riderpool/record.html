On this page

    00 · Why this ledger is device-local

    It is a record, and it is not a backup

    The distinction matters more than it sounds. Everything on this page was written by the calculator on this browser and it will stay exactly there, which is both the point and the limit.

    It is not a backup

    The trips live in this browser's storage under afrik:riderpool:ledger and nowhere else. No account, no cloud, no second device reads it. Clear site data, change phones, or hand the phone to your brother and this table is empty. Export the CSV and keep it where the receipts are.

    It is not a payment record either

    The paid column is a tick you made yourself. Nothing was charged, transferred, held or refunded and no rider was notified. It records who you believe has paid, which is useful at the settlement table and worthless at a bank.

    It is the only price history that describes your corridor

    The reference tables on the data page carry an as-of date and are estimates by construction. Twelve saved trips on one route are measurements. After a season your own cost per seat, your own average load and your own drift are the figures you should be quoting, and the CSV is how you keep them.

    Rounded money, unrounded maths

    Each rider's share is rounded to the naira before it is written down, so a saved trip's seat amounts can fall a few naira short of its cost to cover. The still-owed column therefore works from the trip's own to-cover figure rather than from the sum of the unpaid ticks, so what you are owed stays honest even when the shares round down.

    Sample rows are marked as sample

    Board rows you copy in from the sample list arrive as pending, with seats named Seat 1, Seat 2 or empty and nobody paid. They are fiction that became a row, which is fine for practising a price and useless as a record of a trip. Delete them before the ledger means anything.

    02 · Turning the record into a decision

    What to do with these rows

    A trip record is only worth the decisions it changes. This is the cadence that turns twelve saved trips into a corridor you can price properly.

    Each trip

    Save it the moment it is priced

    Not at CMS. The code, the fare, the method and the seat list are the record, and both the card and the row come from the same inputs, so save before the numbers drift.

    At the stop

    Tick the paid column as money arrives

    The tick is a pencil mark in this browser and nothing more: it moves no money and tells no one. It does turn the row into a settlement sheet you can print and sign.

    Weekly

    Sort by cost per seat and read the column

    Six weeks of your own naira per seat on one corridor is a better fare argument than any single day's arithmetic, and it is the figure that shows whether the asking fare is drifting or the load is.

    Monthly

    Compare trip cost with what you collected

    The totals block gives you net per trip and net per paid rider kilometre. If both are negative every month, the corridor does not pay at your fare and the deficit is not a rounding problem.

    Before you export

    Remember there is no second copy

    The trips live in this browser's storage under afrik:riderpool:ledger. A phone that is wiped, replaced or restored takes the ledger with it. Export the CSV monthly and keep it with the fuel receipts.

    At the argument

    Print the row, not the screenshot

    A saved trip prints with its code, its method and every rider's share. A dispute about a trip that already happened is a dispute about numbers that were written down before it did.
