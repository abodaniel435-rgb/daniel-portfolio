Playbook: for the park, not the office

Argue the arithmetic
before the car moves,
not after.

Nine of the ten fare fights on a Nigerian inter-city run happen because the number arrived late: quoted by habit, defended by temper, settled in traffic. This is the sequence that puts the arithmetic on the table while everybody is still standing at the gate, and the words to use when they disagree with it.

    01 · Before you leave the park

    Ten steps from the question to the signature

    Do them in this order and in this place. Step four after the car is on the expressway is not a calculation, it is an argument with six witnesses.

    1. Someone asks: how did you get ₦9,000?

      Write the question down instead of answering it. 'That is the price' is the sentence that starts every dispute on this corridor. The answer is a sheet with nine lines on it, and the moment you agree to produce the sheet the argument stops being about temperament.

      Say the number out loud and then say you will show the working. It defuses more than any explanation does.

    2. Write the kilometres, not the destination

      Benin City to Lagos is 245 km. Benin City to a gate in Agege on an estate road is 245 km plus a detour plus a queue. The tool loads the corridor distance and the driving hours for you; correct them if your road says otherwise.

      Every later number is a multiple of this one. A distance remembered badly moves everybody's share.

    3. Name the class and take its seat count seriously

      A Sienna is six seats, a Kiss is twelve, a bus is twenty-nine, and the seat count is the denominator under the entire trip. Six bodies plus a crate is not six seats. If the last row is standing, say so and price it as standing, then read step ten again.

    4. Cost the trip line by line

      Litres, then fuel, then wear, then tolls and levy, then the driver's cut. On the worked example that is 27.2 litres, ₦44,917 of fuel, ₦12,005 of wear, ₦8,000 of tolls and levy and ₦25,000 of driver, which is ₦89,922.

    5. Net the parcel money off before you split

      The boot sells before the seats do. Three cartons and a sack is ₦9,500, which takes what the seats must bring in from ₦89,922 to ₦80,422 and cost per seat from ₦14,987 to ₦13,404, and it drops the break-even to nine seats.

    6. Divide, and call it cost per seat

      Cost to cover divided by the units on sale: ₦89,922 over six is ₦14,987 a seat, ₦61.17 a seat-kilometre, a viable fare of ₦15,000. That number is not the fare and not a proposal. It is the thing the fare is supposed to be.

    7. Compare it with the park rate, out loud

      At ₦9,000 a seat the trip breaks even at ten seats and you have six. Say the sentence: the park rate covers 60% of what this seat costs. It moves the argument from who is greedy to what the corridor is worth.

    8. Choose the split method, one, in front of everybody

      Even split, distance share, zone fare, or the park rule of full fare past 60%. The tool scores all four against cost causation and shows the drift in naira per person, average ₦5,155 and worst ₦8,479 for an even split. Show the table before money moves, not after.

    9. Price the detours instead of absorbing them

      Every pickup off the line is kilometres and minutes belonging to everyone aboard. Eleven km is ₦2,556 of fuel and wear, and at the default ₦18 per seat-kilometre it collects ₦198 a seat. Either set the number honest or refuse the stop.

      A diversion agreed in advance is a service. One agreed in traffic is a dispute.

    10. Print the card, sign the manifest, save the trip

      The trip card carries the code and each rider's share. The manifest carries the seat list and a signature box per rider, and that signature is the only receipt in this system. Save the trip and it lands in the ledger on this device, where you can settle it seat by seat.

      Quote the code, not the screenshot. Change a seat, a stop or the fuel price and the code changes with it, which is how two people know they are pricing the same trip.

    02 · What makes a fare a lie

    Eight ways the number you quote stops describing the trip

    A fare is a lie when it is presented as a cost and is not one. Some of these lies are the driver's, some are the passenger's, and most are just habit. The figures are the worked example: 245 km, six seats, ₦89,922.

    • Counting only fuel. ₦44,917 of fuel over six seats is ₦7,486 a seat, and it is half the trip. A fare built on the tank alone cannot pay for the car, the road or the driver, and it is the single commonest reason a full car still loses money.
    • Forgetting wear. ₦49 a kilometre is ₦12,005 on this run, ₦2,001 a seat. It is the line that never arrives as a bill, so it never arrives in the argument, and then the tyres go bald at the exact moment the fare is being discussed. Wear is not a rumour, it is a deferred invoice.
    • Ignoring the return leg. Both legs switched on costs ₦179,843 and ₦29,974 a seat, which nobody pays. Turn on a 50% recovery on the way out instead and the one-way trip becomes ₦134,883, cost per seat ₦22,480, break-even fifteen seats. If the far end has neither load nor cargo, one leg's fare is subsidising the other and only the driver knows it.
    • Splitting evenly across different distances. Six riders, six equal shares, and the man going 77 km pays ₦8,479 more than his kilometres justify while the two going the whole 245 km pay ₦5,719 less. Even is not neutral: it moves money from the short trip to the long one and calls it politeness.
    • Free detours. An 11 km estate diversion is ₦2,556 of fuel and wear and around a quarter of an hour of everybody's afternoon. Absorbing it as a favour means the five riders who did not ask for it paid ₦2,556 between them and never saw it on a sheet. Charge it or refuse it, but do not call it free.
    • Overloading past legal seats. Arithmetically a seventh body is lovely: ₦89,922 over seven is ₦12,846 a seat and everybody smiles. The tool divides by whatever units you enter and will not stop you, because the limit is not arithmetic. Break-even counts seats, not legal seats. If the class says six and eight board, the maths is right and the trip is not.
    • Treating the park rate as a cost. ₦9,000 is a market price and ₦14,987 is a cost. Quoting one as the other is the lie that starts the fight, because both are true at once. The honest sentence is: the park rate covers 60% of what this seat costs, and here is the rest.
    • Rounding the shares before you show them. ₦84.51 a rider-kilometre becomes ₦20,000, ₦19,000, ₦13,000, ₦9,000, ₦20,000 and ₦6,000, and the car is now ₦922 short of its own cost. Round after you show the unrounded table, or the shortfall becomes invisible, which is how it becomes a habit.

    03 · Before anybody boards

    The ten lines to agree in writing

    One sheet of paper, or the trip card on your phone. Nothing here needs a lawyer, it needs a number and a witness. Each line exists because a specific argument happened without it.

    • The corridor and the kilometres are written down. 245 km, one leg, with the pickup point and the drop point named. Not 'Lagos', which is four hours of driving depending on who is guessing.
    • The asking fare and exactly what it covers. ₦9,000, and whether it includes the park levy at the far end. This is the line riders actually disagree about, because both answers are common practice.
    • The split method, stated as two words. Even split, distance share, zone fare or park rule. Whoever says 'we will manage it later' is volunteering to lose that money personally.
    • Each rider's boarding and alighting stop. Not the destination city, the stop. Okela instead of 'on the way' is 3 km of detour and ₦697, priced, instead of an argument at dusk.
    • The detour rule, including the refusal. ₦18 a seat-kilometre or the real cost, plus one sentence about what will not be done: no diversion after dark, no detour over 15 km, nothing that makes the car miss the toll gate window.
    • The departure window and the check-in rule. The card prints it as the departure hour plus or minus the check-in margin, 45 minutes by default. It is the only shared clock six people can rely on.
    • The arrival time, buffered. The ETA chain gives 11:46 raw and 12:21 with a 10% buffer. Give riders the buffered figure. The raw one is a promise you do not control.
    • What happens when a seat does not fill. Every empty seat strands ₦14,987 of cost. Decide now whether the pool absorbs it, the driver absorbs it, or the fare moves, and say it before the car leaves.
    • Parcels, and whose bag is whose. ₦800 to ₦9,000 depending on size, and a signature for the freezer box. Space is the one lever that lowers every fare, so write it down and net it off first.
    • One contact number each way, and the plate. Every rider has a number for the driver and one back home, plus the plate and the seat position. This is the only line on this list that is not about money and it is the one nobody ever regrets.

    04 · For the passenger side

    Signs not to board, and what each one costs you

    The other half of a fair fare is knowing when the trip in front of you is not one. All nine of these are checkable in under a minute at the gate, with the car already loaded and the driver in a hurry, which is exactly when you will not get a second chance.

    • The fare has no working behind it. Ask how it was got. If the answer is a shrug, you are not buying a calculation, you are buying a rumour, and rumours rise fastest when you are the last seat sold.
    • More bodies than seats. Count them, including the lap. A six-seat car with eight aboard is not generosity and not experience, it is the trip's whole surplus spent in advance on people who have no seat.
    • The belts are missing, cut, or already used up. One per body is not a formality. If the middle row has two belts for three people, the fare you are arguing about is being paid for by a risk you are carrying.
    • A tyre that is cord, patched on the flank, or mismatched. The spare counts too, so ask to see it. A car with one bad tyre and a full load is a trip whose cost sheet forgot the most expensive line of all.
    • The driver has not slept, or will not say when he last did. A 4am departure on a 470 km run is a schedule built by the cheapest person in the car. A night surcharge is ₦2,500. Fatigue is not priced in this tool because it cannot be.
    • Nobody can tell you where you alight. If your drop is 'along the expressway', your kilometres are unknown, your share is unknown, and the detour argument at dusk is already decided against you.
    • The money is collected before the manifest exists. Cash first, no slip, no name, no signature box. The manifest's signature is the only receipt in this system and it is worth asking for by name.
    • The detour list is longer than the route. 25 km of diversion on 245 km is a tenth of your trip spent buying other people's pickups. Ask which stops are detours and who is paying for them.
    • The car is being loaded past the boot. A freezer box on laps is not a parcel, it is a second fare for the same seat plus the weight to match. If the boot has become a seat, the seat count was a guess.

    05 · The day of the trip

    One run, timed against the tool's own ETA chain

    The sample board leaves Benin park at 06:00 in a Sienna with six seats on sale and all six filled. The clock times below are the tool's chain at 62 km per hour with a 10% buffer and six minutes allowed at each stop where somebody boards or alights. They are not a forecast and the toll queue is in none of them.

    05:15

    At the park, cost sheet done

    The trip is priced before the car is loaded, because loading is when the fare conversation starts and the fare conversation is when the car leaves late.

    Copy the card before the first passenger arrives, not after.

    05:45

    Board, check, mark the manifest

    Names, phones, seat position, boarding and alighting stops, and who has paid. Three riders board at the park and they bring ₦60,427 of the ₦89,922 with them.

    06:00

    Wheels-off, km 0

    Six seats, no parcels, and a check-in window on the card running 06:00 plus or minus 45 minutes, which is the only workable version of 'he is coming'.

    06:43

    Okela, km 18, 3 km off the line

    One rider boards. ₦697 of detour cost at ₦232.33 a kilometre and 13 minutes of the run: early, cheap, and the sort of detour everybody forgives.

    08:18

    Ore, km 85, 5 km off

    ₦1,162 of fuel and wear and the confluence point of the run. Everyone in the car pays this whether or not the pickup pays it, which is why the pickup is charged and not thanked.

    08:49

    Ijebu-Ode, km 108, on the road

    The cheapest stop shape there is: one rider boards, no kilometres diverted, 11 minutes. If a detour is worth doing it usually looks like this instead.

    09:50

    Sagamu, km 130, 11 km off, nobody boarding

    The estate set-down: ₦2,556 of real cost, ₦0 collected at this stop, 24 minutes. This is the stop that decides whether your detour rule was honest.

    10:48

    Iyana Ilogbo, km 185

    One rider alights after 77 km. Under an even split he paid ₦14,987 for a ₦6,507 trip; under distance share he paid ₦6,507. This is the moment the method becomes visible to him.

    12:21

    CMS, km 245, buffered arrival

    The card said 11:46 unbuffered and 12:21 buffered, and the buffered figure is the one riders should have been told. The far-end levy belongs in the fare line, not in this minute.

    That afternoon

    Settle, save, then price the return

    Tick the paid column as money arrives, export or photograph the manifest, save the trip so it lands in the ledger. Then decide what the empty leg home costs, because ₦29,974 a seat is the answer if nobody loads for you.

    06 · Questions people actually ask at the gate

    Straight answers, no hedging

    Why should the man going to Ogba pay less than the man going to Ibadan?

    Because he rode less of the road. Fuel and wear are consumed per kilometre, and on this trip the running cost is ₦232.33 a kilometre, so the last 20 km costs ₦4,647 that the early alighter never used. He does consume something fixed though: the tolls, the levy and the driver's cut are paid once for the whole trip, which is why a pure distance split is not automatically right either. That tension is exactly what the four methods model differently, and the tool shows you both in naira, per person.

    Is the detour fare legal?

    This calculator does not know and will not tell you. Routing, permits and fare caps are decided by state law and by park practice, and they differ from place to place. What it does tell you is the arithmetic: 11 km off the line costs ₦2,556 in fuel and wear and about a quarter of an hour of everybody's time, and the default ₦198 a seat recovers less than half. Charge what the detour costs and agree it with the riders already aboard before you turn off.

    What about the return leg when I have no passengers?

    Switch both legs on and the same trip costs ₦179,843, which is ₦29,974 a seat and no conversation anyone wins. The honest options are a return load you find before you leave, or the recovery percentage on the outward fare: at 50% the one-way cost becomes ₦134,883 and cost per seat ₦22,480, which is what a Sienna actually needs on a corridor where the far end has neither cargo nor passengers.

    The park says ₦9,000. Your arithmetic says ₦14,987. Who is wrong?

    Neither. One is a market price and one is a cost, and the gap between them is why cars sit at the park waiting for a tenth seat and why some trips are simply losses a driver takes and quietly recovers next month. If a corridor cannot pay ₦14,987 a seat, the answer is not to insist the seat costs ₦9,000. It is to sell more seats, fill the boot, or stop running that trip in that class.

    Do I have to fill all six seats for the fare to be right?

    For the trip to break even at ₦9,000 a seat you need ten seats and you have six, so no: a full car still leaves ₦35,922 short, and each further empty seat strands ₦14,987 more. The break-even figure is the reason this page exists.

    The last row is standing. Is that a seat?

    Arithmetically the tool will treat it as one, because it divides by the units you enter, and a seventh body does lower everybody's cost per seat to ₦12,846. Physically and legally it is not, and the tool will not stop you because it is not in a position to. What you may carry is decided by the class of vehicle and the rules on it, not by a calculator.

    Someone boarded at Ore and will not pay. What can I do now?

    Nothing this tool can do. Marking a seat paid is a pencil tick in your own browser: it moves no money, contacts nobody and holds nothing. The manifest's signature box is the only receipt in the system, which is why it is signed at the stop rather than settled at CMS.

    Can you find me four more riders?

    No. There is no pool, no queue, no notification and no matching of any kind. The sample board the tool ships with is fiction written into the source file. Real pooling is a phone call and a park agent; this page's only job is to make the numbers inside that call checkable.

    Why does the front row cost more than the middle?

    Because it is worth more to somebody: legroom, the view, and being first out at every stop. It is 8%, ₦720 on a ₦9,000 fare. Note that the front-row passenger is usually also the navigator, which is a job rather than a seat, so decide whether the ₦720 buys the position or the help. Those two arguments get mixed constantly and the mixed version always favours whoever is collecting.

    Is my passenger list uploaded anywhere?

    No. Names, phone numbers and the manifest live in this browser on this device and nowhere else. That is also what makes the ledger fragile: clear site data and your saved trips are gone. Export the CSV if the record matters to you.

    The code changed when I edited the fuel price. Is that a bug?

    It is the feature. The code is derived from the route, the date, the class, the seat count and the trip cost, so a change to any of them changes it. Two people quoting the same six characters are pricing the same trip, which is the only reliable way to know that the ₦9,000 you both mean is the same ₦9,000.

    Which method should I actually use?

    The tool ranks distance share first, then even split, then the park rule, then the zone fare, scoring average drift plus 35% of the worst drift plus a penalty for the shortfall. But that ranking assumes riders will accept ₦20,706 for a full-length seat, and sometimes they will not. Pick the most honest method the car will actually run, write it on the sheet, and let the fare be what it is.

    07 · Words on the card

    Glossary

    Every term the tool and this playbook use, in roughly the order you meet them at the park.

    Trip cost
    Fuel plus wear plus tolls and park levy plus the driver's cut, plus any night bonus and recovery percentage you switch on. On the worked example: ₦89,922 for Benin City to Lagos, one leg, in a six-seat Sienna.
    Cost to cover
    Trip cost less whatever the boot brings in. The money the seats alone must produce. Parcels are netted off before any split is computed, which is why a full boot lowers everybody's fare rather than only the driver's mood.
    Cost per seat
    Cost to cover divided by the units on sale: ₦14,987 in the worked example. Not a price and not a proposal, it is the figure the asking fare is being compared against.
    Viable fare
    Cost per seat rounded up to the next ₦100, so ₦15,000 on the worked example. The lowest fare that covers the trip at the current seat count, and it moves the moment a seat, a stop or a litre price moves.
    Break-even seats
    Cost to cover divided by the asking fare, rounded up: ten seats at ₦9,000. When that figure is higher than the seats you have, the corridor does not pay at that price and the car is being run on hope.
    Cost causation
    The yardstick every split is scored against. A rider's fair share is the cost to cover multiplied by the kilometres he rode, divided by everybody's kilometres. ₦84.51 a rider-kilometre on the sample board.
    Drift
    The naira difference between what a method charges a rider and what cost causation says that rider owes. Reported per rider, as an average and as the worst case, because the average hides the one person who is angry.
    Stranded cost
    Unsold seats multiplied by cost per seat. ₦14,987 for every empty seat in the worked example: the money that stays on the floor of the car whether or not anyone notices.
    Detour charge
    What a pickup off the main line costs each seat per diverted kilometre, ₦18 as shipped. On an 11 km diversion that is ₦198 a seat against ₦2,556 of real fuel and wear, so the default is a contribution and not a recovery.
    Stop economics
    Per stop: the detour kilometres and minutes, what they cost the trip, who boards and alights, the fare brought in there, and the net. Two stops on the sample run are negative because they divert and nobody boards.
    Recovery percentage
    A line added to the trip cost to pay for something the trip does not earn directly, usually the empty return leg. It is applied to fuel, wear, extras and the driver's cut before any split.
    Buffer
    The percentage added to raw driving time to produce the arrival time riders are told. 10% as shipped, working from the average speed you set and the minutes you allow per stop.
    Check-in window
    The departure hour plus or minus a margin, 45 minutes by default, printed on the card. It is the only shared clock six people agree on.
    Trip card
    The plain-text sheet with the code, the money lines, who pays what, and the arrival figures. Copy it into the group chat. It is text, not a booking.
    Manifest
    The printable seat list: name, phone, position, boarding and alighting stop, kilometres, what is owed, paid or owing, and a signature box. The signature is the receipt.
    Trip code
    Six characters derived from the route, date, class, seat count and trip cost. Same inputs, same code; any change changes it. Quote the code, not the screenshot.
    Ledger
    Every trip you saved, in this browser only, with its seat list attached. Sortable, exportable, and emphatically not a backup.
