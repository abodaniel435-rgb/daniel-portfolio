The spread is not
your profit.

A ₦19,000 per crate gap between Zaria and Mile 12 survives only if the trip, the levies, the porters and the spoilage leave something behind. Most losses are decided in the first five minutes at the gate, before any truck is hired, by whether you asked about the unit.

    01 · Before the money moves

    How to read a price board, eight steps

    Run them in this order. Step one costs nothing and it is the one everybody skips, which is why a ₦1,100 mudu and a ₦26,000 crate get compared as if they were the same object.

    1. Turn every quote into kilograms

      Ask what a crate, basket, mudu, keg or tubu weighs, then divide. Two offers are only comparable when they describe the same mass. Section 03 of the tool ranks them for you once you have the weights.

      If the seller will not say the weight, the weight is the problem.

    2. Name the tier, not the price

      Farm gate, wholesale desk or city retail. The same crate is legitimately three different numbers, and a buyer quoting you retail at a wholesale gate is testing whether you know.

    3. Ask what the grade is

      Mixed grade, single size, old lot on top of new. A price under the band is usually a grade answer, not a bargain answer.

    4. Write the four invisible costs

      Trip, levies, porters, spoilage. All four are per load or per unit, and all four are the reason a wide spread produces a thin profit. The haul calculator will not invent them for you.

    5. Compute the break-even quantity

      Contribution per unit is (1 minus spoilage) times the sell price, less the buy price, less the porter fee. Fixed cost divided by that is the number of units that must go before you earn anything. Ask for it before you agree to fill a truck.

    6. Check where the month sits

      Index at or above 1.12 means the calendar is already paying the seller who holds. Index at or below 0.88 means the glut is here and storage is the only way to turn it.

    7. Decide hold or sell in writing

      One line, before the buyer arrives: the price you take today and the price you will not go below. Section 06 of the tool turns it into an anchor, a target and a walk-away.

    8. Log the trade the same day

      Date, commodity, direction, quantity, unit, price per unit, market. Undated trades are not a record, and a cooperative cannot split what nobody wrote down.

    02 · The cheap crate

    Nine things that make a cheap crate expensive

    Each one of these shows up as a low number on a good. None of them is a discount.

    • Mixed grades. Two layers of good fruit over a base of soft and split ones. The crate is priced like the top layer and filled like the bottom one.
    • The wet crate. Rinsed produce looks fresh and weighs more. You buy water at the price of tomato, and it drips away before your stall.
    • Crate weight counted as goods. Close to 2 kg of the 22 kg is wood. A heavy crate is a discount on the mass you can actually sell.
    • The under-volume mudu. The opposite of the heaped mudu: a filled calabash that is short because the measure was never struck. Same price, less grain.
    • The paint bucket pretending to be a keg. 16 litres of garri is about 9 kg; the 25 litre keg is 25 kg. Per unit they look close. Per kilogram they are not the same trade.
    • Porters at both ends. Loading, offloading, and again to move the crate from the truck to the stall. The calculator charges ₦300 once per unit carried, so a 350 crate load is ₦105,000. If your desk pays twice, say so before you compare two prices.
    • Levies you only meet on arrival. Union, association, local government, and the stops on the road. The board puts the default at ₦12,000 for a trip precisely because nobody writes the real list down.
    • Spoilage in the queue. A fifth of a soft load is gone before the last crate is opened, and more of it goes while you wait for a buyer. Loss is not only the road, it is the afternoon.
    • Moisture in the bag. Grain at 16% moisture is 50 kg partly made of water, and it cakes in storage. You pay for the water twice: at the gate and again when the buyer rejects it.

    03 · Hold or sell

    The decision, in the order you make it

    Not a forecast. A sequence of checks that ends with an answer you can defend at home.

    First

    Can it survive the wait?

    Leaf, soft produce and fresh goods do not have a storage option, whatever the index says. Grain, dry goods, oil and tubers do. Ask this before you ask about price.

    The board carries a crop class for exactly this reason.

    Index 1.12 and above

    Sell

    You are already standing in the lean month. Holding into a peak you are inside buys you nothing and costs you storage, financing and risk.

    Index 0.88 and below

    Buy, or hold if it stores

    This is the glut trough. It is the month to buy for later, or to sit on your own harvest, provided the class survives four months and you dry it properly.

    In between

    Let your own costs decide

    Near the annual average the calendar gives you no edge. Grade, freshness and your landed cost are the whole argument, so price the trip instead of the month.

    Before a buyer

    Compute the break-even rise

    Storing pays only after loss, storage cost per unit per month and financing. The tool gives you the sell price you must reach; if that price is above the lean-month figure on the data page, the plan is hoping, not holding.

    Under 20%

    Thin is a warning

    A return inside the noise of one rat, one flood, one week of rain on the road is not a profit. Store only what you can protect for free, in a shed you already own.

    Never

    Do not store on moisture

    Grain above roughly 13-14% does not lose money later, it gets rejected. Drying is not a cost line, it is the permission to store at all.

    04 · Sell at a loss

    Warning signs that you should move today at whatever the day pays

    A loss you take on purpose is a decision. The same loss discovered at the end of the season is a habit.

    • The contribution per unit is already negative. Sell price after spoilage, less buy price, less porters, below zero. No quantity of load fixes that, so do not hire the truck.
    • Your quantity is below break-even and you cannot fill. The first units only pay the driver. If the load will not close the gap, sell at the gate and keep the trip cost.
    • The crop class is soft or leaf and the market is two days away. You are buying a depreciation schedule with a transport bill attached.
    • The index is falling from its peak. Yesterday's lean month is today's slide. A month that is high and dropping is worse than one that is low and rising.
    • Storage cost plus financing is over a third of the monthly gain. You are paying somebody else's shed and somebody else's money to take the risk yourself.
    • You already have stock of the same good. A second lot of the same crop is not a hedge, it is one more thing that has to move before it turns.
    • The buyer wants credit. Sell forward means sell now at a lower price. If it also means paid later, you have taken the discount and kept the risk.
    • Rain is coming to the road you must use. Spoilage, delay and a levy list that grows with the number of days. Move early or move less.
    • You cannot say your landed cost per unit. If the number is not written down, the price you accept today is a feeling, and feelings lose money in a market.

    05 · The same day you buy

    The ten-line checklist

    Ninety seconds at the gate. Every line has saved somebody a trip they should not have made.

    • You weighed or counted one unit yourself. One crate or one mudu, out of the lot, in front of you.
    • Three desks quoted, not one. Price per unit and per kilogram, at the same tier, in the same hour.
    • The tier is named on paper. Farm gate, wholesale or city retail. A farm gate price you pay at a retail desk is not a bargain.
    • Grade checked in the middle and at the bottom. Not at the top, which is where the good fruit lives by design.
    • Trip cost confirmed with the driver, including the return leg. Fixed figure or rate per kilometre, one of the two, written before loading.
    • Levies and porters listed for both ends. The ₦12,000 and ₦300 defaults on the board are a reminder, not your actual figures.
    • Spoilage entered as a number you will own. The class rate is a starting point. Use your own once you have logged three trips.
    • Break-even quantity computed before you commit. If your load is below it, the answer today is the gate price.
    • Walk-away price decided before the buyer opens. Below cost plus your minimum margin, you are financing somebody else's margin with your own capital.
    • The trade logged the same day, with the market named. This browser is not an archive, but a dated row is evidence at a union meeting.

    06 · Questions traders actually ask

    Straight answers, no hedging

    The same crate of tomato is ₦9,000 at the gate and ₦28,000 in Lagos. Why is that not a good business?

    Because ₦19,000 is a spread, not a margin. On the board's own example, 350 crates, each crate keeps about ₦13,100 after spoilage, the buy price and porters, and the trip plus levies take ₦202,000 off the top. That is still a strong trip, which is exactly why the arithmetic is worth doing before the money moves rather than after.

    How many crates do I need before a haul pays at all?

    Break-even quantity is trip cost plus levies divided by contribution per unit. On the Zaria to Lagos tomato example the tool says 16 crates: the first sixteen only pay the driver and the levies, and everything past that is yours. It is computed live from whatever you typed.

    Is a mudu cheaper than a bag?

    Ask in kilograms. A struck mudu is about 0.35 kg and a bag is 50 kg, so one bag is roughly 143 mudu. If the bag costs less than 143 times the mudu price, the bag is cheaper, and usually the roadside mudu is the dearer way to buy grain.

    Why does my per kilogram price change when the crate is wet?

    Because the mass changed, not the money. A 22 kg crate carrying 2 kg of water is 20 kg of goods at the same price, so your real cost per kilogram rose by about 10% while the sticker stayed.

    Should I sell my yam now or wait for February?

    February is the board's lean month for yam, which is why it is the dear month. Whether waiting pays is not a calendar question: it is loss in store, storage cost per unit per month, financing, and whether your tubers hold their count. Put those four into the store panel and read the break-even sell price.

    What is a fair porter fee?

    Whatever you wrote down before you argued. The tool's default is ₦300 per unit because that is the order of magnitude at a city retail gate, and it is charged per unit carried, not per unit sold. Confirm it at both ends, because loading and offloading are two charges with one name.

    Can this tool tell me tomorrow's price?

    No. It tells you what your own numbers imply today, and what the seasonal shape implies about direction. Weather, a road closure, a fuel increase, a border, a buyer with better information than you and a union dispute all beat the calendar.

    The band says my quote is below the bottom. Is that a bargain?

    Either it is, or the unit is short, the stock is old, the grade is mixed with the previous lot, or it is not the commodity you think. Weigh it and check the middle of the crate before you smile.

    Do I need to log trades I have already paid for?

    Log them anyway, with the date you remember. A buy and a sell of the same good is what lets the record page show your realised margin against the board. Without pairs, you are guessing whether the year was good.

    07 · Words at the gate

    Glossary

    Every term the tool uses, in the order it appears in your own arithmetic.

    Unit cost
    Price divided by the kilograms in the unit you were quoted. The only figure that makes two offers comparable. The board's 50 kg beans bag at ₦105,000 is ₦2,100 a kilogram, so a struck 0.35 kg mudu of the same beans is worth near ₦735. At ₦1,100 you are paying half again as much and being told it is a small sale.
    Farm gate
    The price where the crop is loaded, before any road. The board's reference gates are Zaria, Baguda, Kano and Nsukka.
    Wholesale desk
    An assembly or offload market that resells rather than consumes. Berkiji Ojo and Agbowo sit here, which is why their multipliers are under 1.
    City retail
    The stall, priced by rent, levy culture and porter habit. Mile 12 is the board's reference desk at ×1.00.
    Gross spread
    Sell price per unit less buy price per unit, with nothing subtracted. The number that makes a bad trip look like a good idea.
    Contribution per unit carried
    (1 − spoilage) × sell price − buy price − porter fee. What one more crate actually adds before the fixed costs.
    Break-even quantity
    Trip cost plus levies, divided by contribution per unit, rounded up. Below it the load only pays the driver.
    Break-even haul distance
    How far the same load can travel before the trip eats the contribution: (contribution × units − levies) ÷ rate per kilometre. The tool reports the quantity version; divide your fixed cost by the rate to get the distance.
    Landed cost per unit sold
    Total outlay divided by the units that survive to be sold. Spoilage shows up here as an inflated goods cost, which is why it is the honest number to negotiate from.
    Margin per crate
    Sell price less landed cost, on one crate. Not the same as spread, and usually less than half of it.
    Seasonal index
    One month's price as a multiple of the annual average, drawn as 1 ± the swing peaking in the crop's lean month. Index 1.28 is the top of the default swing.
    Lean month
    The month a stored crop is worth most, before the new crop arrives. September for maize, millet and garri; February for yam and onion; December for eggs and chicken.
    Glut month
    The harvest trough, when everyone is selling the same week. October and November on the board for most grains. It is a buying month, not a panic month.
    Sell forward
    Agreeing today's delivery at a later month's price, usually below the lean figure, so the buyer carries the wait. You take a discount to remove risk; if payment is also later, you have removed none of it.
    Market multiplier
    A desk's cost of doing business as a factor on the commodity's own tier price. Editable, and the reason the same crop is three prices in one city.
    Levy
    Union, association and local government charges, plus the stops on the road. Charged per trip or per unit, always collected, almost never written down in advance.
    Porter fee
    What it costs to move a unit from truck to stall and back again. Per unit, both ends, and the smallest number on the sheet that changes the answer.
    Storage loss
    Weight gone to weevils, drying, sprouting, rats and theft while a good sits. The board plans 4% on grain in a sack, 10% on yam and cassava, 20% on a hot crate of tomato and 25% on leaf that wilts inside a day.
    Financing cost
    What the money you tied up costs you per month, whether it is cooperative savings, a 50:50 advance, or simply what you could have bought instead.
    Realised margin
    What your logged sells actually returned over your logged buys, per commodity, goods only. Transport, levies and spoilage stay in the haul calculator, so it flatters you unless you remember that.
