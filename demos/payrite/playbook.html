Playbook · for the owner who signs it

Payroll is a process,
not a calculation.

The maths is the easy half. Most penalties come from a missed date, a wrong base or a new starter whose TIN never arrived. This is the sequence that keeps a small business clean, month after month.

    01 · The monthly sequence

    Eight steps, in this order

    Run them in this order and every figure downstream has one source of truth. Out of order, you will reconcile a number that was already wrong.

    1. Freeze the package definition

      Basic, housing, transport, other — written once, applied to everyone. If a role is paid differently, the role is different, not the person.

      Change this only at the annual review, never mid-month.

    2. Collect the employee file

      TIN, bank details, RSA number, next of kin, and the rent position if you intend to claim rent relief. A missing TIN is the most common reason a PAYE return bounces.

      New starters: file before the first pay run, not after.

    3. Enter the package once

      In PayRite, one row per salary level. Use the staff multiplier for identical packages instead of typing the same figure twelve times.

    4. Apply reliefs deliberately

      CRA is automatic. Rent relief is a choice with a document behind it — if you cannot show the rent, do not claim the relief.

    5. Compute PAYE on the taxable figure

      Gross, less CRA, less rent relief, less the employee pension. Then slice across the bands. Anything that taxes gross is wrong.

    6. Reconcile the employer cost

      Net pay is not the cost. Add employer pension, employer NHF, group life premium and NSITF before you decide whether the hire is affordable.

    7. Pay, then remit

      Pension within seven working days, PAYE by the 10th. Pay the employee first; the deductions are held on trust, not owned by you.

    8. Close the month with a record

      Export the roster as CSV, keep the payslips, and note the two numbers that moved. Twelve months of that is your annual return.

    02 · What actually goes wrong

    Six mistakes, ranked by what they cost

    • Pension on gross. The base is basic + housing + transport. Charging 8% of gross over-deducts the employee and under-funds the RSA.
    • Flat 24% or flat 7%. Neither is the law. Marginal banding is what makes the number defensible.
    • Rent relief without rent. Claiming 20% of a rent the employee does not pay is an assessment waiting to happen.
    • PAYE filed to FIRS. It goes to the state board where the employee works. Wrong authority, unpaid liability.
    • Ignoring the employer side. A 10% pension and 2.5% NHF on top of every salary changes your hiring decision, not just the slip.
    • No paper for new starters. Paying before the TIN and RSA exist means a correction you will make under time pressure.

    03 · Before you press pay

    The one-page checklist

    Take ninety seconds. Every line here has saved somebody a penalty.

    • Every employee has a TIN. Including the one who joined on Monday.
    • The package total matches the contract. Not the last payslip — the contract.
    • Pension base is basic + housing + transport. Check one row by hand if you changed anything.
    • Rent relief is off unless documented. The switch in the calculator is off by default for a reason.
    • Net plus every employer cost equals the bank instruction. Reconcile the total, not the count.
    • The CSV export is saved somewhere you will find in March. This browser is not an archive.

    04 · The payroll calendar

    What lands when

    A year of payroll is a repeating calendar. Write these five dates on the wall and most of the panic disappears.

    Day 1

    New starter paperwork

    TIN, RSA, bank letter, next of kin. Anything missing today becomes a correction next month.

    Day 2–5

    Run the pay computation

    One entry per package, staff multiplier for identical roles, worked steps reviewed before anyone is paid.

    Day 7 working

    Pension remittance

    Both the employee 8% and the employer 10% to the PFA. Late payment attracts a penalty that is not negotiable.

    Day 10

    PAYE to the state board

    Return and remittance together, per employee, per state. Keep the acknowledgement.

    Month end

    Payslips and the log

    Issue slips, export the roster CSV, note the two figures that moved since last month.

    March

    Annual reconciliation

    Twelve months of the log against the board's assessment. Discrepancies here are cheaper than queries later.

    05 · Questions owners actually ask

    Straight answers, no hedging

    Do I have to deduct PAYE if the business is small?

    Yes, if you employ people and they earn above the relief threshold. Being a small company affects company income tax, not your duty as an employer to withhold an employee's tax.

    What if an employee earns less than the minimum wage?

    They should not exist. The national minimum wage is a floor, and PAYE on a package at the floor is usually close to zero after reliefs — compute it anyway and keep the record.

    Can I pay the tax myself and recover it later?

    You can bear the cost, but the withholding obligation stays yours. Recovering it from the employee after the fact is where payroll disputes start.

    Two staff, same salary, different PAYE. Why?

    Reliefs. One of them may have documented rent, a different pension base, or a mid-year raise. The bands are identical; the taxable figure is not.

    Does a consultant need PAYE?

    Generally no — a genuine consultant invoices you and may attract withholding tax instead. The test is control and integration, not what the contract is called. Get this one wrong and it is expensive.

    What happens if I miss the 10th?

    Your board can impose a penalty and interest on the amount late, and the employee's record shows a gap. Pay it immediately and keep proof of the correction.

    Should I use this tool to file?

    Use it to compute and to check somebody else's computation. The filing itself goes through your board's portal, on their form, in their format.

    Is the employer cost really 13% more than net?

    Often more. Employer pension 10% and NHF 2.5% of the package, plus group life and NSITF, on top of gross — the calculator shows the exact figure for your own package.

    06 · Words you will meet on a payslip

    Glossary

    Every term, in the order it appears on a Nigerian payslip.

    PAYE
    Pay As You Earn — the employee's income tax, withheld by the employer and paid to the state board monthly.
    CRA
    Consolidated Relief Allowance: ₦200,000 plus 20% of annual gross, subtracted before the bands apply.
    PITA
    The Personal Income Tax Act, the law that sets the bands, the reliefs and the withholding duty.
    Taxable income
    Gross less CRA, rent relief and the employee pension. The figure the bands are applied to.
    Marginal rate
    The rate that would apply to the next naira earned. Not the rate applied to everything.
    Effective rate
    Total tax divided by gross. The number that tells you what pay is actually costing you.
    PRA
    Pension Reform Act 2014 — the 8% and 10% contributions and the RSA structure.
    RSA
    Retirement Savings Account, held with a Pension Fund Administrator, one per employee.
    NHF
    Nigeria Housing Fund: 2.5% employee and 2.5% employer, recoverable as a housing loan.
    NSITF
    Nigeria Social Insurance Trust Fund: 1% of payroll for workplace injury cover.
    TIN
    Tax Identification Number. Without it a PAYE return cannot be matched to the employee.
    Consolidated earnings
    Basic + housing + transport. The base most statutory deductions are actually calculated on.
