01 · The problem

The panic is not the fees. It is the timing.

Nigerian households are not uniformly poor and uniformly rich — they are paid monthly and charged termly. That mismatch is where the damage happens: 10–20% a month to a salary-advance lender, trading stock liquidated at a loss, or a child sent home for an unpaid balance weeks before the exams.

01 / Ibadan

A teacher and a trader, three children

One salary, one business that swings with the season. Public school fees are small, but PTA dues, exam fees, uniforms and new books every September stack into a real number — and all three children resume in the same week.

02 / Lagos

A single mother, JSS fees in 5 weeks

The letter arrives on a Friday: ₦268,500 before resumption. Five weeks is enough to raise it in instalments if somebody asks — and not nearly enough if nobody does. Asking is the part most parents were never shown how to do.

03 / Kaduna

A civil servant paid late, again

Income arrives, just two or three weeks after the school expects it. The advance that bridges the gap costs more per year than the fees themselves — and the only cure is a plan the bursar has already agreed to.

    09 · Assumptions, in the open

    Every number this tool uses, and you can change.

    Nothing is hidden. Edit a band and the planner, the sinking fund, the comparison and the letter all follow. Restore the defaults whenever you want to see the indicative figures again.

    Fee bands by school type (editable)
    Editable fee bands
    BandFees / termTermly extrasYearly extrasTerms

    Bands are national indications drawn from the ₦120,000–₦1,200,000 a year range private schools charge, and from the levy stack on top of public school fees. Your school's letter beats this table every time.

    Inflation and terms

    School-fee inflation defaults to 20% a year (editable in section 03); the realistic band is 15–25%. Basic education runs three terms, universities and some federal schools run two sessions; extras charged once a year are divided by that number.

    Salary advance and loan maths

    A "₦80,000 for 30 days at 15%" advance costs ₦12,000 for the month. Annualised the way lenders actually mean it — rolled every month — that is (1 + 0.15)12 − 1. FeesPlan shows both the compounded figure and the flat ×12 figure so nobody can hide behind either.

    Ajo / esusu circle risk

    Each member pays the same amount every week and one member takes the pot each week, so the circle charges no interest. The two real risks are timing (your turn falls after the fee deadline) and gaps (a member misses your week). Coverage without a buffer is (1 − gap)^(members − 1); asking for one spare week squares the failure chance instead of removing it.

    What is deliberately not modelled

    No school's actual fee schedule, no guarantee that any school accepts instalments, no loan approval, no investment return, and no state-level subsidy or free-education scheme eligibility. Ask your school and your state's SUBEB office about the last one.

      10 · Honest limits

      What FeesPlan cannot tell you.

      Fees vary enormously

      By state, by city, by street. A standard private school in Ibadan and the same brand in Lekki are different economies. Every band here is indicative and every total must be checked against your school's written fee schedule.

      This is not an offer of credit

      FeesPlan does not lend, brokering nothing, and moves no money. The advance and instalment percentages illustrate the products parents are actually shown at the gate and on the phone. Confirm any rate in writing before you sign.

      Circles depend on people

      The ajo risk figures assume each member misses independently. Real circles fail together — one salary delay becomes three. Treat the coverage percentage as the optimistic case, and keep the ledger in public.

      Letters are not legal advice

      The draft is a plain request between a parent and a school. The withheld-exams note lists steps to try, in order. It is not advice, and it cannot read your state's education law for you.

      Nothing leaves this page

      Saved plans, the ledger and your edits live in this browser's storage. Clear your site data and they are gone — export the CSV before you change phones.

      The only certain number

      Is the one the bursar writes down and signs. Ask for the fee schedule in writing, ask what the instalment policy is, ask what happens after week six. FeesPlan turns the answers into a plan.
