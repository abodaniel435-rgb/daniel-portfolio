Playbook · between your gate and his scale

Sorting is twenty minutes.
It is also the whole margin.

The material is the same either way. What changes is the grade the buyer sees, the weight he writes down and the price he offers. This is the order of work that keeps all three in your favour.

On this page

    01 · The sequence

    Nine steps, in this order

    Do them in order and the pile you hand over is the pile you priced. Out of order, you are negotiating a number you cannot explain.

    1. Name the load before you touch it

      Type it into the counter the way you will remember it: Block C, Saturday sweep. A school or association should name the cycle or term. An unnamed load is a line nobody will defend at a meeting.

      The label travels with the record into the ledger.

    2. Make three piles first: plastic, fibre, metal

      Not seven. Three. The first cut is what stops a ₦300 aluminium can being paid at ₦45 because it was buried in carton.

    3. Split the plastic by resin

      Clear PET, coloured PET, HDPE jerrycans, sachets, and whatever is left as mixed. Clear PET is ₦120, HDPE ₦200, mixed ₦45. One bag of mixed is the difference between paying for a trip and not.

    4. Split the fibre by grade and keep it off the floor

      White carton and newsprint at the top, brown corrugated below, kraft sacks apart. Never on wet ground, never under a leaking roof edge, never tied to a wet sack.

    5. Dry what you can and stop adding water

      Rinse once, drain, then sun it. Sachets and milk pouches are worth 15 and 12 naira a kilogram wet or dry, but the deduction on wet fibre is real and it also drops the grade.

    6. Crush and tie

      Crushing does not change a gram. It changes how many kilograms fit on a keke, which changes whether the trip clears break-even at all.

    7. Count the pieces, then weigh once

      Run the numbers in the counter. It gives you the kilograms your pieces imply. That figure is the expected weight in the ledger and the only thing that can argue with his scale.

    8. Test and watch the scale

      Zero it, put your 1 litre bottle on it (1 kg), take the crate off, and stand where the dial is visible. If the crate stays on the load, declare the tare before he writes the number.

    9. Agree price and deduction, then log it in front of him

      Say the rate per kg, the per-piece items, and the wet deduction out loud. Then write date, material, kilograms, price, naira earned, buyer's name and whether you saw the scale into the ledger. The record is made at the scale, not at home.

    02 · Where the money quietly leaves

    Seven traps, and what each one costs

    • Mixed grades priced as the lowest grade. Green bottles in clear PET, tin cans in aluminium, glossy inserts in newsprint. One handful of the wrong thing reprices the whole sack, and it is not even dishonest - that is how he buys.
    • Wet carton counted as dry. The 8% deduction lands on a weight that is already inflated by water. You are paid less for material that weighed more, twice.
    • Un-crushed volume priced as weight. Standing boxes fill a keke at a quarter of the kilograms. Same trip cost, one quarter of the money, and he will blame your material.
    • A scale you cannot see. Behind the cab, on the lorry, weighed after the load is off the ground and out of sight. Anything not visible is not verified, and the ledger's checked column exists for exactly this.
    • A tare nobody agreed. The crate comes off once and then again as 'the bag weight'. Declare your own tare before he writes anything, and put it in the note.
    • Per-piece items weighed, weighed items priced per piece. A laptop board is a per-piece line; 2.1 kg of it at a mixed-plastic rate is a robbery nobody has to say out loud. Know which of your items are priced by the kilogram and which by the piece.
    • No receipt, no note, no name. Three months later nobody remembers a ₦2,400 load. The buyer's name and the reading are what turn a memory into a claim.

    03 · Reading the buyer

    Signs you are being shorted, not unlucky

    One of these is a bad day. Two, against the same buyer, is a pattern. Put the pattern in the ledger and take it to the association meeting.

    • His scale reads your 1 kg water bottle at 900 g. That is not a rounding error, it is a calibration, and it is in your favour to find out now.
    • Every load of yours is a bit wet. Yours is damp and everyone else's is dry. Weigh it yourself before he arrives and compare.
    • He offers a round total instead of kilograms times a rate. ₦1,800 for the lot. Refuse the round number and make him say the weight and the rate.
    • The mixed rate only ever moves down. Copper and aluminium get quoted daily; your fibre rate has not changed in four months while his price lists have.
    • The weighing happens after the material leaves your side. Once it is on his lorry the second opinion is his, not yours.
    • He deducts for your own sack, and takes the sack. Declare the tare, then get your bag back. Sacks and rope are a real monthly cost.
    • He objects to a notebook. A man with an honest scale has never once minded you writing down the reading.

    04 · Before the material goes on the scale

    The ten-second checklist

    Take it in this order. Every line here has saved somebody a trip or a fight.

    • Piles are separated and named, nothing mixed on top of anything. Plastic by resin, fibre by grade, aluminium away from iron.
    • The counter already says what this load should weigh. That number is your expected kilograms. If you have not counted, count now.
    • Your 1 litre bottle of water is in your hand. One kilogram, sealed, undeniable.
    • The scale is zeroed, on hard ground, and you can read it. If any part of that fails, say so before the first sack goes up.
    • The rate, the deduction and the per-piece items are said out loud. Kg times rate, plus tyres and boards at their own prices.
    • Your crate or bag weight is declared, and you are keeping the bag. Once, not twice.
    • Date, buyer's name, the reading and whether you saw the scale go into the record. Written before the money moves.

    05 · The collection week

    The rhythm that makes sorting free

    Sorting is only a chore when it happens in one panic before the keke arrives. Spread across the week, it costs nothing, and each day has one job.

    Monday

    Set out the day's material and name it

    A gate collection on a tricycle needs bags ready at a window people actually keep. Whatever you cannot sort today goes in a named pile, not in a mixed sack.

    Tuesday

    Split the week's plastic by resin

    Clear PET, coloured PET, HDPE, sachets. Twenty minutes with a radio on, and the mixed pile stops existing.

    Wednesday

    Weigh the depot's reading against your count

    The miles depot is open early. Take one bag, weigh it where you can see, and note the gap. This is how a rigged scale gets caught cheaply.

    Thursday

    Carton day, head load or market line

    Fibre is the one material rain can ruin in an hour. Tarp it, raise it off the ground, and sell it the same day it dries.

    Friday

    Check the trip maths before you call anyone

    Schools and institutions: the truck wants hundreds of kilograms. Below break-even, hold it and combine with another household rather than pay for an empty run.

    Saturday

    The estate round, and the ledger entry

    Best carton day in most estates. Weigh, agree, log, and export the CSV before the weekend swallows the numbers.

    Sunday

    Nothing moves

    Quiet hours. Use ten minutes to read the week back: kilograms, naira, and any row flagged as a dispute.

    06 · Questions people actually ask at the gate

    Straight answers

    How many bottles make a kilogram?

    About 33 of the 1.5 litre size, or 100 of the 500 ml size, since they weigh roughly 30 g and 10 g. Milk pouches are about 12 g, so 83 of them make a kilo. Count the pieces and you already know what the scale should say.

    Do I need to buy a scale?

    No. The item count gives you the expected weight, and a sealed 1 litre bottle of water gives you a 1 kg test you can put on his scale. Weigh one bag at the depot once a month if you sell regularly; that single habit changes how carefully you are weighed.

    Does crushing bottles raise my price?

    It raises your money per trip, not per kilogram. The rate is on the kilogram and crushing changes no grams - but a keke full of un-crushed bottles carries a quarter of the load for the same ₦120/km trip cost.

    He says my carton is wet and I know it is not.

    Ask him for the gross, the tare and the deduction in writing. Then weigh it yourself. The tool's default carton deduction is 8%, so on 40 kg the argument is about ₦112, which is worth standing still for.

    Is ₦45 a fair mixed rate?

    It is the default in the tool because it is a common quoted floor for unsorted plastic and paper. Your buyer's number is the only relevant one. Put his number in the price table, keep the as-of date, and let the comparison run on his figures.

    My load does not clear the trip. What is the actual advice?

    Do not call yet. Either hold the material for another week or combine with the households the scheduler says you need. Below break-even the collector loses money on the run, and he recovers it from your price, not his fuel.

    He paid me less than my calculation said. What do I do?

    Log the weigh-in: the kilograms he sold you against the kilograms your count implied, plus his name. The ledger flags the row once the gap passes your tolerance, which is 12% by default. One flag is an argument; a month of flags against the same buyer is a case.

    Should I count tyres and e-waste in the kilograms?

    They are priced per piece, so they add weight to the load but their money comes from the piece count. A phone at 180 g and ₦700 a piece is not a plastic sale; a truckload of tyres is a lot of weight and very little money.

    Why does glass sometimes cost me money?

    Because plenty of buyers charge to take it. The price table allows negative numbers on purpose, and the counter subtracts honestly. If glass is on your load, ask before you carry it.

    Does this ledger prove he cheated me?

    No. It proves what you recorded, which is a stronger position than memory but not evidence. Weigh on a visible scale or refuse the load. The tool gives you the number; the leverage still has to be you, standing at the scale.

    07 · Words on the ledger and at the depot

    Glossary

    These are the field names the tool uses. Learn them and the record reads itself.

    Sorted
    Material separated by type and grade, presented that way. Priced per material: PET clear ₦120, HDPE ₦200, aluminium ₦300.
    Mixed
    Everything in one sack, priced at one low rate. The default mixed rate in the tool is ₦45/kg.
    Uplift
    Sorted value minus mixed value for the same load. It is what your twenty minutes of sorting actually returned.
    Expected kg
    The kilograms your item count implies. Stored as expectedKg on every record, and the second opinion his scale has to answer to.
    Kg sold
    The weight that was actually paid for, his reading. Stored as kg, and always the number money was calculated on.
    Earned
    The naira you received for that row: kg times price, or the per-piece total. Stored as earned, in naira, rounded to whole naira.
    Checked
    Whether you saw the scale: yes, no, or shown to me but not verified. The difference between a measurement and a story.
    Dispute
    A record where the kilograms you sold sat more than your tolerance below the kilograms your count implied. Flagged automatically, and it is the row you take to a meeting.
    Contamination deduction
    The percentage a buyer takes off the rate for wet, oily or dirty material. It sits on each row of the price table and it is editable, because every buyer applies his own.
    Tare
    Crate, sack or rope weight taken off the load. Declare it once, in writing, and get your bag back.
    Trip cost
    One-way kilometres times the vehicle's naira per km. Push cart ₦40, keke ₦120, 3-ton truck ₦320 in the tool's defaults.
    Break-even weight
    Trip cost divided by the price per kilogram on the table. Below it the pickup loses money and someone has to absorb that, and it will be you.
    Blended rate
    Sorted value divided by total kilograms: the honest naira-per-kg your actual mixed-material load earns, not a single material's price.
    Per-piece pricing
    Tyres, phones and laptop boards sold by the unit rather than the kilogram. Weight is still counted in the load, because it still fills the truck.
    Aggregator
    The big yard the collector finally sells to. Their shortage is what moves your price, which is why a price list without a date is worthless.
