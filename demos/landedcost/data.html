What sits between the
invoice and the shelf.

You are quoted one number and you pay six different bases. Duty sits on the assessable value, the surcharge sits on the duty, VAT sits on everything added so far, NIMASA sits on the freight, and the terminal sits on the box. Here is each one, with the naira result for the worked example on the calculator page.

    01 · Six bases, not one

    What each charge is actually calculated on

    Most arguments at the agent's office are about the base, not the percentage. A percentage applied to the wrong base is the whole reason a quotation and an assessment disagree.

    Assessable valueduty, ETLS, CISS and stamp are charged on it
    The duty itselfthe surcharge is 20% of this line
    CIF + duty + leviesthis is what VAT is charged on
    The freightNIMASA and the Shipping Council take 3% of it
    The box, or the CBMterminal handling ignores the value of the goods
    167 kg per CBMthe air volumetric divisor: chargeable weight is the greater

    03 · Port, documents and lines

    Filter the kind column and look at what is left. A fixed charge is only small on a big container, and a percentage is only honest about the value you declared. Most surprises are fixed charges nobody quoted.

    04 · The clock

    Free days, brackets, and the day a cleared box becomes a loss

    Demurrage escalates in weekly brackets once the free period is spent, and the shipping line bills container detention alongside it. Both are per box. Your own money sitting in the goods is the third clock and the one nobody invoices.

    One container, 7 rent-free days, detention at ₦12,000 a day from the day after the free period, capital carried at 4% a month on a landed cost of ₦18,617,835. Gross profit on the load, at a 35% target margin, is about ₦6,516,000.

    Detention ends when the empty box is back with the line, not when your truck leaves the gate. Groupage cargo is charged cargo rent instead of these brackets, so ask the consolidator for the free period in writing.

    05 · Incoterms

    Where your responsibility starts under each term

    The term on the proforma decides two things at once: who pays for each element below, and what ends up inside the value customs assesses. Getting it wrong either inflates your duty or leaves your own freight out of the arithmetic.

    You = invoiced to you. Seller = included in the price quoted to you. Only the elements marked yes enter the assessable value that duty, ETLS, CISS and stamp are charged on.

    The tool's own warning, repeated here: adding freight and insurance to a CIF or DDP invoice a second time inflates the duty you model by 15 to 30%.

    06 · Where these numbers can still be wrong

    Three places a correct calculation still meets a different invoice

    The value is contested, not the rate

    An assessment can substitute its own figure for your invoice value. No table on this page anticipates that; it is a revaluation, and it can exceed every margin figure the tool shows.

    Terminal and line tariffs move without notice

    THC, delivery orders and council charges are revised by operators. Update the defaults in section 10 from the invoice you were actually handed, not from a page, including this one.

    The rate you priced at is not the rate you fund at

    The naira on the day the bank releases is the naira that matters. The percentage columns above are stable; every naira figure in them is not.

    07 · What to check these against

    Documents, not opinions

    • The proforma invoice and the supplier's packing list: carton count, carton dimensions and gross weight per carton.
    • The bill of lading or airway bill, for the freight, the Incoterm actually shipped on, and the discharge date that starts your free days.
    • The Form M / MACIS printout and the bank's rate, for the naira figure you will really convert at.
    • The assessment on the file: line by line, with the base of each line shown, so you can compare it with the trail in section 04 of the calculator.
    • The terminal's current tariff card and the shipping line's free-time and detention schedule, both of which change more often than the duty bands do.
    • The SONCAP or product certificate for each invoice line you are paying the per-line fee on.
    • Your own last three landed costs in the shipment log, which is the only table that describes your supplier and your agent rather than somebody else's.
