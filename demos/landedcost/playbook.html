Playbook · before you transfer anything

The margin is decided
before the goods ship.

Nothing at the terminal can give you back what the quotation gave away. This is the order of work that keeps a consignment profitable: value it, choose the term, book the freight, plan the clearing days, then price the shelf off the landed number and check what naira rate kills it.

    01 · The order of work

    Ten steps, in this order

    Each step has one input that the next step depends on. Doing step four before step three is how people discover the container was the wrong structure after the freight was booked.

    1. Get the packing list before the price

      Carton count, carton dimensions, units per carton, gross weight per carton. Volume and weight drive freight and handling; the invoice value does not.

      A supplier who cannot give dimensions is a supplier who will surprise you.

    2. Read the Incoterm on the proforma

      EXW means everything from the factory door is yours. FOB means your freight and insurance become part of the customs value. CIF and DDP mean they are already inside the price, and adding them again inflates duty by 15 to 30%.

    3. Convert at the rate you will fund at

      Not today's rate on a screen: the rate your bank will actually release at, for a file that may be funded weeks from now.

    4. Build the freight from the CBM

      Carton dimensions times cartons gives cubic metres. Groupage is priced per CBM and stops being cheaper at about 38 CBM, where a whole 20ft box costs the same. Air is priced on chargeable kg, the greater of actual weight and CBM times 167.

    5. Get the HS line, then set the band

      Ask the agent for the HS line and the duty percent in writing. The category list in the tool is a starting point; the line is the truth.

    6. Add the fixed charges yourself

      Form M, the agent's fee, SONCAP per invoice line, the box charges if you ship FCL, NIMASA on the freight. They are small individually and they are the first thing a freight quote forgets.

    7. Plan the clearing days before the vessel sails

      Pre-register the file, know the free period from the line, and know who signs what. The clock is measured from discharge; your documents should be ready days before it.

    8. Add the local tail

      The truck is a base plus a per-kilometre rate, then porters, then storage if the goods will not move. A container tractor with a permit is a different decision from a 3-ton truck.

    9. Divide by the units you can sell

      Landed cost divided by invoiced units is a lie once breakage and shrinkage are counted. At 1.5% the cost per sellable unit is already higher than the cost per shipped unit, and the break-even shelf price is above that.

    10. Price it, then price the risk

      Set the shelf price, read the profit per unit and on the load, read the carry cost over the days of credit, then read the naira rate at which the margin disappears. If you cannot survive that rate, the shipment is a currency bet with cartons in it.

    02 · Where the profit goes

    Seven traps, and what each one costs

    None of these are exotic. They are the seven things importers argue about at the terminal, and every one of them is visible before you order.

    • Quoting a FOB price as if it were landed. On the worked example the landed number is about 53% above the invoice. Anyone pricing from the supplier's figure has already lost that much and does not know it yet.
    • Pricing at the rate you ordered at. Duty, levies and VAT all scale with the naira rate, so an 8% move is not 8% of your profit, it is 8% of almost the whole cost. Only your fixed charges stay where you left them.
    • Assuming the free days are still there. The period is measured from discharge, and a vessel that waits days at anchor arrives into a backlog. Ask the line what is left of the free period on the day the box lands, and write the answer down.
    • Carton weight versus carton volume. Air charges the greater of the two. Groupage charges the volume. A container of light bulky cartons fills its cube and pays handling on air nobody can sell.
    • 'Duty paid' slips that are not receipts. A total on a piece of paper is not an assessment. Ask for the line-by-line with each base shown, and read it against the trail in section 04 of the calculator before you pay the balance.
    • Counting freight and insurance twice on a CIF or DDP invoice. The seller already paid them, so they are inside the value you were quoted. Adding them again raises the assessable value and every percentage charged on it.
    • The all-in clearing promise. One figure for everything, no breakdown, no receipt. Under-declaration and the informal channel are how consignments get impounded, and neither is a cost saving. Nothing here models them.

    03 · Before you order

    The pre-order checklist

    Ninety seconds, on every new consignment. Each line is a number you will otherwise be told after the money has left.

    • Carton count, dimensions and weight are from the packing list. Not from the sample, and not estimated from the photos.
    • Two freight quotes on the same CBM: groupage and one 20ft. Whichever is cheaper, plus the box charges if it is the box.
    • The HS line and the duty percent, in writing. From the agent, before you price, not after the assessment.
    • SONCAP status for each invoice line. The per-line fee is real and it multiplies.
    • The free period from the shipping line, and the detention schedule. Days, not adjectives, and which clock each one belongs to.
    • A named truck and the distance from the port to your store. Base plus per kilometre. The gate is not the end of the cost.
    • Days to sell and days of credit you can actually finance. If the carry costs more than the gross profit, no shelf price saves it.
    • A market price you have checked on the street, not in your head. Enter it as the price the market will bear and see whether it clears break-even.
    • The naira rate at which the margin disappears, written next to the order. That number is the risk. If it is below the rate you fear, do not place the order.

    04 · The shipment calendar

    What happens when, and what is priced at each point

    An import is a sequence of dates, and the cost lines attach to the dates rather than to the goods. Work backwards from the day the box leaves the terminal.

    Day 0

    Proforma invoice and packing list

    Cost it here, before anything is committed. The landed number you produce today is the price you can still negotiate from.

    Save the shipment in the calculator; you will re-run this file three times.

    Week 1

    Form M and the bank

    The application goes in, the naira rate becomes real, and the amendments you did not expect start. Nothing on the water moves until this exists.

    Week 1 to 2

    Payment or acceptance

    Money leaves, or your credit line does. From here the capital clock is running whether the vessel is or not.

    Week 2

    Booking and documents

    Freight confirmed, original bill of lading issued, SONCAP or product certificate in place per line. The invoice you book with is the invoice customs reads.

    Weeks 4 to 8

    On the water

    The only useful work left is preparation: pre-register the file, book the truck, get the evacuation permit, and confirm what is left of the free days.

    Arrival

    Discharge, and the free period starts

    Day 1 of the clock that matters. Documents ready means the assessment can be raised the same week.

    Free days, then brackets

    Clearing, assessment, duty and VAT paid

    Inside the free period this costs you days of capital. Outside it, it costs ₦18,000 a day a box, then ₦32,000, then ₦48,000, then ₦62,000.

    Release

    Evacuation, empty box return, delivery

    Detention runs until the empty is back with the line, not until your truck is gone. Return the box, then finish the paperwork at the agent's office.

    Days to shelf

    Sell it, then count

    Compare what the load actually returned with the margin and ROI the log said it would. That difference is next order's correction.

    05 · Questions importers ask

    Straight answers

    Is the CIF value the same as the invoice value?

    Only under CIF or DDP, where the seller's price already contains freight and insurance. Under FOB your own freight and insurance are added to the goods value to reach it, and under EXW your origin haulage joins them. On the worked example: USD 8,000 of goods at ₦1,520 is ₦12,160,000 of goods, and the assessable value is ₦14,470,400.

    Do I really pay duty on my freight?

    Yes, under EXW and FOB, because freight and insurance are part of the customs value. That is why a cheap freight rate makes the whole shipment look cheaper than it is, and why NIMASA at 3% of the freight is charged where the freight is small.

    Why is VAT charged on more than the CIF?

    Import VAT is charged on the duty-inclusive value: CIF plus duty, surcharge and levies. So a higher band raises the VAT as well as the duty, and a valuation increase raises both too.

    When does demurrage start counting?

    After the free period, measured from discharge. The tool's defaults assume seven days and then weekly brackets at ₦18,000, ₦32,000, ₦48,000 and ₦62,000 a day a box. Confirm the free period with the line for your specific consignment; it is not the same on every service.

    What is the difference between demurrage and detention?

    Demurrage is the charge for the box and the goods sitting at the terminal past the free period. Detention is the shipping line's charge for holding their container beyond the allowed time, and it stops when the empty box is returned. They run at the same time and they are billed separately.

    My agent's figure is bigger than the tool's. Which is wrong?

    Neither, probably: one of you used a different rate, a different band, or a per-line SONCAP count. Ask for the assessment line by line, put each base into the trail, and find the row where the two of you differ. That is a faster argument than disputing a total.

    Can I take the VAT back out of my cost?

    If you are VAT-registered and the goods are for business use, import VAT is input VAT you can reclaim rather than cost, and the tool has a switch for exactly that. Until the refund actually reaches you, treat it as money you have lent the system.

    How much naira movement can a shipment survive?

    The tool solves it directly. On the worked example, priced at a 35% target margin, the margin disappears somewhere near ₦2,080 to the dollar: about 37% above the rate quoted at. Run your own file and write the number down before you order.

    LCL or a full container?

    Compare them on the same CBM. At ₦38,000 a CBM, groupage costs as much as a whole 20ft box at ₦1,450,000 once you pass roughly 38 CBM, and past that point a container also stops the per-CBM warehouse handling. Below it, groupage is the right structure; do not pay for air you cannot fill.

    Should I quote my customer in dollars?

    You can, but then you have moved the FX risk to your customer and the collection risk to yourself. If you price in naira, price off the landed number and re-test the shelf against the break-even rate every time the naira moves.

    06 · Words on the file

    Glossary

    Every term the calculator and the assessment use, in the order you will meet them.

    CIF
    Cost, insurance and freight. The invoice figure that already contains the freight and the cover, and the name people give to the customs value even when the goods were bought FOB.
    Assessable value
    The value customs charges duty and the levies on. Built from the goods plus freight plus insurance, plus origin costs if you bought EXW. Also called the CIF value on most files.
    Duty-inclusive value
    The assessable value plus duty, surcharge and levies. The base VAT is applied to, which is why VAT moves whenever the band moves.
    Break-even shelf price
    The shelf price that recovers the landed cost per sellable unit and the output VAT on it, and earns nothing. Anything below it is a loss wearing a profit.
    Margin disappearance rate
    The naira rate at which landed cost per unit equals your net selling price. Above it you are paying the difference out of your own pocket. The calculator solves for it in section 08.
    Demurrage
    The terminal's charge for days past the free period, per container, escalating in brackets.
    Detention
    The shipping line's charge for holding the container past the allowed time. It ends when the empty box is returned, and it runs alongside demurrage.
    Free days
    The rent-free period, counted from discharge rather than from the date on the schedule. The single most valuable and most assumed number in an import.
    Incoterm
    The three letters on the proforma that decide who pays each element and what ends up inside the assessable value. EXW, FOB, CIF and DDP are the four the calculator models.
    Form M
    The import application that puts a foreign purchase on record through a bank, and the document whose amendments cost the most days.
    SONCAP
    The product conformity certificate charged per invoice line, the absence of which stops goods at the port rather than at the office.
    CISS
    The surveillance fee taken as a percentage of the assessable value.
    ETLS
    The community levy, also charged on the assessable value, and one of the small percentages that nobody argues about.
    THC
    Terminal handling charge: a flat fee per container that does not care what is inside it, which is why a half-empty box is the worst cost per carton you can buy.
    CFS
    The container freight station where groupage cargo is handled, charged per cubic metre instead of per box.
    Chargeable kg
    For air, the greater of actual gross weight and volume weight, which is CBM times 167. Light and bulky is expensive.
    Uplift
    How far the landed cost sits above the invoice, as a percentage. The first number a customer disputes and the one you should already know.
    Carry
    The cost of your own money while the goods are unsold or unpaid, at the monthly rate you entered. It is not on any invoice, and it comes off the profit quietly.
