Reference · the numbers you are expected to edit

Every figure the calculator
will not invent for you.

FuelMeter computes from reference values you can see and change. Here they are in full: thirteen state pump rows, the consumption each vehicle class is assumed to have, what servicing, tyres and oil cost per kilometre, and the fare comparators drive-versus-hail runs on. Check them against the pump you use and the mechanic you trust, then edit them inside the tool.

These are reference values with an as-of date you set: there is no live feed and no pretending.

    Add the scheduled rows except oil and you get roughly ₦10.50 per kilometre on a small petrol car, about ₦14 once you include labour at every visit.     09 · Where these numbers get argued

    Three places the table and the road disagree

    The pump row you did not update

    The ₦80 spread across thirteen states is smaller than the difference between the row and the price at your station this morning. Set the as-of date in the tool and type the number from the display, then price your trip.

    The claimed km/L nobody achieves

    Profile figures are the open-road number owners repeat. Loaded, with A/C, in city stop-go, a Corolla is nearer 10 than 12. The log is how you find your own number instead of borrowing one.

    Wear averages that fit somebody else's car

    Servicing, tyre and oil lines are amortised averages. Your actual set of four, your actual mechanic and the suspension work you keep postponing will differ. Depreciation is not in any of these tables at all.

    10 · Check these against

    Five places worth opening before you change a number

    • The pump display and the receipt from the station you actually use, against each editable state row and its as-of date.
    • Your own odometer and your own litres. Two consecutive full-tank fills give a km/L figure that describes your car, not the catalogue.
    • The placard inside the driver's door for tyre size and cold pressure, and the sidewall of the tyre you are standing next to.
    • Two or three mechanics for the same job on the same car. The spread between their quotes is more useful information than the cheapest one.
    • The fare table in the calculator, which is yours to edit: minimum, per-kilometre rate, and whether you are being charged for a vehicle or for a seat.
